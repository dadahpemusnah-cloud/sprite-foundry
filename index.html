Pivot

    Per-frame data for game engines, in frame pixels from the top left (margin included). Exported in the atlas JSON (meta.frameData), the manifest and the Godot and Unity packs. Hitboxes are red, hurtboxes green, the pivot is a cross.

    Layers · top first

    Layers are the sprite's parts; each moves as one piece in the animations. New pixels join the selected layer. Hidden layers are hidden here and protected from edits; they still export. Moving a layer changes the draw order everywhere.

    2 · background

    Automatic uses the image's transparency if it has any, otherwise GrabCut. GrabCut separates busy backgrounds but is not perfect: fix the result with the brush and run it again.

    Brush

    Keep: paint over the character (green). Remove: paint over background (red). The brush always wins; GrabCut also learns from it when you run it again.

    3 · pixel art

    The sprite is size × size px with a 2 px margin (frame size + 4). Headroom is free space above the head for raised weapons and jumps. Downsample: average keeps smooth shading; majority keeps flat colour regions crisp.

    Palette

    Use

    Makes this pixel art the Imported sprite in the editor, timeline and exports. Each use is kept as a version you can return to.

    4 · body parts

    Paint a short stroke inside each part, then Grow from strokes. Pink pixels belong to no part yet. Brush, lasso and fill assign the chosen part; the eraser unassigns.

    L and R mean the screen side (left and right as you look at the picture).

    Back limb

    When the image shows only one arm or leg clearly, a darkened copy can stand in for the one behind the body. It is marked user-made: the image does not show that limb.

          6 · animation

          Rig preflight

            Checks the parts, joints and rest pose before you animate. "Needs attention" items do not stop you; each button opens the step that fixes it.

            Animations move the parts you made: they cannot turn the character round or show sides the image does not have. A walk seen from the front lifts the feet instead of swinging the legs. Edits to the base pose in the editor do not reach animation frames; edit a frame ("This frame") or use step 7.

            Key at this frame

            Pose

            Drag on the preview to turn the chosen part around its joint. Nudges are in quarter pixels, but a part that is only nudged, or turned so little that no pixel moves half a pixel, is drawn moved by whole pixels (so it keeps its exact pixels); the whole body moves in whole pixels. Editing a frame between keys makes a key there. A drawing (step 7) replaces the part from this key until the next key.

              Secondary motion

              The chosen part (Pose, above) trails behind the parts it hangs from: it also turns by the amount × how far they turned some frames ago. For a cape, hair or a tail. It is added to the part's own keys.

                Frames edited by hand

                Edited frames keep their pixels when keys change. Re-rendering replaces them with the rig's frame.

                Prepared motions for your character's rig: timed poses for its joints, drawn with your own parts by the rig renderer. Nothing is generated or invented. Preview a motion, adjust it, then add it as a new animation that you can edit in Manual.

                Character preview

                Direction

                  Each direction is its own image. Add one in Image to pixel art (Direction, at the top); set which way the main image shows there too. Sprite Foundry never rotates, stretches or mirrors a flat picture to make a direction.

                  Template motions

                  Motion settings

                    7 · drawings and hidden areas

                    Everything drawn here is yours, not the image's, and the frame check marks frames that show it. Alternate drawings are for poses a rotation cannot make (a fist, a foreshortened arm). The hidden area is what is under a part where another part covers it in the image; it can only be painted there (pink squares), so it never shows at rest.

                    2.5D Sprite Workshop · draw one sprite by hand, frame by frame

                    A flat pixel-art sprite drawn at an angle to look three-dimensional. It is not a 3D model: each view is drawn by hand. Separate from the generator and the image import: nothing here changes them.

                    Live preview: keeps playing while you draw.
                    Rotation preview: every direction with its own artwork, in step.

                    Brush: none yet. Select pixels, then Brush from selection; the Brush tool (K) stamps them, held at their centre, with the mirror and, if ticked, the dither.

                    Select drags a rectangle; drag inside it to move its pixels. Copy and Paste work across frames (the selection's layer). Sel-out turns a flat outline of the current colour into darker shades of what it encloses.

                    Highlight lightens and Shadow darkens each pixel one step, to the next colour of its ramp in the palette. Light the sides that face the sun marker; shade the sides away from it.

                    Reference image

                    An image shown under your drawing to trace over. It is saved with the project and never drawn into frames or exported.

                    Replace the current colour with

                    Keys: B pencil, E eraser, G fill, I eyedropper, H highlight, S shadow, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo. Changing the palette never repaints pixels already drawn.

                    Animate with the rig

                    Sends the frame shown (every layer, as drawn) to Sprite Foundry's Image to pixel art as a new image: its pixels are kept one for one (conversion "exact", the palette locked to its colours). Then press Use as sprite in step 3, mark the body parts and joints (steps 4–5), and Template Motion (step 6) can animate your drawing. The drawing here is not changed.

                    Export sprite sheet (transparent background)

                    Columns 0 puts every frame in one row. Each frame sits in its cell centred, feet on the bottom row (the pivot is the bottom centre). All three layers are exported, also ones hidden in the editor.

                    UI slices

                    A slice names a rectangle of the sprite (a button, a panel). Insets make it a 9-slice: the corners stay as drawn, the edges and the middle stretch. Slices go into the JSON (meta.slices) and each becomes a Godot StyleBoxTexture (.tres) in the ZIP.
